# 14 wonders of Gothic (and Neo-Gothic) architecture:

1\. Leuven Town Hall, Belgium

Built during the middle of the 15th century, this is perhaps the greatest of the many great Medieval town halls throughout Belgium and the Netherlands.

In few other places did secular Gothic architecture achieve such grandeur and refinement. [![](../img/000d/F2UCDbgXwAA3UjA.jpg)
](../img/000d/F2UCDbgXwAA3UjA.jpg)

2\. Chhatrapati Shivaji Terminus, Mumbai

One of the major buildings of the 19th century Gothic Revival, this rail station mixed both Medieval Italian and various schools of traditional Indian architecture into a style called Bombay Gothic.

Also a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

3\. Sainte-Chapelle, Paris

Built by King Louis IX and completed in 1248, the Sainte-Chapelle is a miracle of the uniquely French Rayonnant Gothic style.

And its stained glass windows are almost entirely original, having somehow survived centuries of warfare and revolution. [![](../img/000d/F2UKJUFWUAA56IR.jpg)
](../img/000d/F2UKJUFWUAA56IR.jpg)

4\. Hungarian Parliament Building, Budapest

Overlooking the Danube and resplendent inside and out, this is still the largest building Hungary.

Imre Steindl, the architect, went blind shortly before it was completed in 1902 — he never saw his Neo-Gothic masterpiece finished.

5\. Lonja de la Seda, Valencia

Built during the early 16th century in a Late Gothic style unique to Valencia — notice the twisted columns — this was neither a church nor a palace but a commercial centre and silk exchange, where merchants gathered to trade and do business. [![](../img/000d/F2UGyRnXgAA-7iv.jpg)
](../img/000d/F2UGyRnXgAA-7iv.jpg)

6\. Strasbourg Cathedral, France

Started in the 11th century and completed 400 years later, Strasbourg Cathedral unites four centuries of French Gothic architecture.

Once the tallest building in the world, it is now the tallest surviving structure built during the Middle Ages.

7\. Belfry of Bruges, Belgium

Home to 47 bells which still ring out over the city, this colossal tower has been partly or wholly rebuilt three times down the centuries because of damage caused by lightning.

The lower half is from the 1200s and the octagonal part from the 1400s. [![](../img/000d/F2UG7TKXAAABFrF.jpg)
](../img/000d/F2UG7TKXAAABFrF.jpg)

8\. Lincoln Cathedral, England

Originally a Norman church from the 11th century, it grew throughout the Middle Ages into one of Britain's greatest cathedrals.

In 1311 it overtook the Pyramid of Giza as the world's tallest building — until its spire fell down in 1548.

9\. Vladislav Hall, Czechia

Gothic architecture developed along different lines in Central Europe, where a unique style known as "Sondergotik" emerged, exemplified by the sinuous, flowing vaults and broken ribs of the huge Vladislav Hall, built in Prague Castle in the late 1490s. [![](../img/000d/F2UHE9rWQAAcDQ8.jpg)
](../img/000d/F2UHE9rWQAAcDQ8.jpg)

10\. Cathedral of Learning, USA

Built between 1926-1934 for the University of Pittsburgh, it is still the second-tallest university building in the world.

And though it only loosely draws on Gothic architecture, in terms of ambition it matches the cathedrals of the Middle Ages. [![](../img/000d/F2UHHrHWoAAcfIP.jpg)
](../img/000d/F2UHHrHWoAAcfIP.jpg)

11\. Orvieto Cathedral, Italy

Possibly the most striking façade of any Italian cathedral, this cornucopia of sculpture, mosaic, and carving was designed by the renowned architect and artist Orcagna.

It also shows how Italian Gothic architecture was distinct from northern Europe. [![](../img/000d/F2UHKn1XwAELQ2k.jpg)
](../img/000d/F2UHKn1XwAELQ2k.jpg)

12\. Tribune Tower, Chicago

A major international competition was held for its design in 1922; some of the submissions were way ahead of their time and anticipated modern skyscrapers.

But the winner was Raymond Hood, with a Neo-Gothic design based on Rouen Cathedral in France. [![](../img/000d/F2UK9j_WsAESlJX.jpg)
](../img/000d/F2UK9j_WsAESlJX.jpg)

13\. Ely Cathedral, England

One of the most striking and unusual of all Medieval cathedrals. What you see here is the underside of the octagonal lantern (which lets light into the nave) that sits atop Ely Cathedral, surrounded by an exuberant array of painted stone vaulting. [![](../img/000d/F2UjmM8W8AAPsmT.jpg)
](../img/000d/F2UjmM8W8AAPsmT.jpg)

14\. Hohenzollern Castle, Germany

The ancestral seat of the noble House of Hohenzollern, what we see here is actually the third castle built on this hilltop, constructed in a fairytale Neo-Gothic style for Frederick William IV of Prussia during the middle of the 19th century. [![](../img/000d/F2UkNkjWIAA1HgN.jpg)
](../img/000d/F2UkNkjWIAA1HgN.jpg)
